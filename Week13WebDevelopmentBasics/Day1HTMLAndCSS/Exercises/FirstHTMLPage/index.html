The domestic cat

The domestic cat is a small member of the cat family that lives alongside people. It may spend its day exploring, sleeping, grooming and playing. Even a well-fed cat can show hunting behaviors such as stalking and pouncing.

Characteristics

  • Whiskers help a cat sense nearby objects.
  • Most claws can retract when they are not being used.
  • Sharp teeth and a flexible body help it catch small prey.

Behavior

Cats communicate through body posture, scent and sounds. An upright tail, flattened ears or a hiss can each convey something different. Many cats hunt on their own, while cats living near one another may form social groups.

Meet a larger relative, the lion

The lion

The lion is a large wild cat. Lions share familiar feline behaviors, including grooming, resting and stalking prey, but their size and social life set them apart from the domestic cats that live with people.

Characteristics

  • A muscular body and powerful jaws help a lion tackle large prey.
  • Adult males often have a mane around the head and neck.
  • Its tawny coat blends into dry grass and open landscapes.

Behavior

Lions often live in groups called prides. Pride members may rest together, groom one another and cooperate when hunting. Roaring helps them communicate over longer distances and signal their presence to other lions.

How they compare

Characteristics and behavior of the domestic cat and lion
Trait Domestic cat Lion
Size Small enough to live comfortably in many homes. A large, muscular wild cat.
Habitat Homes and areas close to people. Wild habitats including grasslands and open woodland.
Hunting Usually hunts small prey alone. Can hunt large prey alone or with other lions.
Social life Varies between independent living and social groups. Often lives with other lions in a pride.
Signals Uses meows, purrs, scent and body language. Uses roars, other calls, scent and body language.
